Abstract
Urban centers face increasing pressure to achieve carbon neutrality. This thesis develops a Python-based analytical model that couples CMIP6 climate projections with borehole heat exchanger physics, quantifying how a warming subsurface reshapes the geothermal heat-extraction potential across Germany under SSP 2-4.5 and SSP 5-8.5 scenarios, over 50-year and 100-year sustainable operating horizons.
§ 03 - Key results

Warming substantially boosts geothermal yield.

Each degree of subsurface warming effectively substitutes ~4 metres of drilling depth, a measurable economic and physical lever.

SSP 2-4.5 by 2100
+1.7°C

Mean shallow ground temperature rise under modest mitigation (±0.6 °C across the CMIP6 ensemble).

SSP 5-8.5 by 2100
+3.1°C

Subsurface warming under high-emissions trajectory (±1.1 °C ensemble spread).

Extraction efficiency
+8-24%

Increase in geothermal heat-extraction potential by 2100 vs. current baseline conditions.

Drilling equivalent
~4m / °C

Each 1 °C of warming substitutes roughly 4 m of borehole depth for equivalent heat supply.

§ 02 - Workflow

From climate model to borehole physics.

Four-stage pipeline integrating CMIP6 ensembles, FLS-based heat-transport modeling, and interactive geospatial visualization.

-> 01 / DATA

CMIP6 acquisition

8 GCMs from Google Earth Engine under SSP 2-4.5 & SSP 5-8.5; reprojected to EPSG:4326.

-> 02 / MODEL

FLS heat transport

Finite Line Source theory (Rivera 2017) with superposed surface boundary heating.

-> 03 / SOLVE

Brent's iteration

Root-finding for sustainable extraction rate at minimum fluid temperature.

-> 04 / VISUAL

Live JS rendering

Per-pixel data exported as JSON, rendered with Leaflet + chroma-js for live colormap switching.
